# Pilot data retention and account erasure

Canceling a Stripe subscription pauses automation; it does not request erasure. Past-due accounts keep read-only data access and billing. An explicit account deletion immediately disables the business and revokes its sessions, then enters a durable `deleting` state. The privacy worker retries incomplete deletion. Operators monitor `MaintenanceTaskHeartbeat` and deleting accounts until completion.

Deletion first verifies Twilio number release and Stripe subscription cancellation. Google grants are disabled locally in a transaction and placed in an encrypted revocation outbox; final erasure waits for verified revocation. A provider outage leaves the account disabled, with encrypted retry credentials excluded from ordinary reads. Reconnect is blocked until revocation completes. Never discard an uncertain provider receipt to force completion.

After external receipts, a Mongo transaction enumerates physical collections and erases records matching the business (ObjectId or string), businessId or targetBusiness. If this is the owner's last business and the owner is not an administrator, it also erases the owner, authentication challenges and user-linked support data. Future child collections using these canonical ownership fields are covered. Tenant data must never be stored under arbitrary ownership field names. Another tenant's records are never erased. A failed transaction rolls back all local erasure.

Hashed per-tenant provider-budget counters and reservations are explicitly erased too; shared fleet totals remain as unlinked aggregate spend and are never refunded by deletion. Budget records otherwise expire after the centrally fixed 100-day spend-reconciliation period (`PILOT_PROVIDER_BUDGET_RETENTION_DAYS`); an owner cannot shorten it to reset a cap.

Removed data includes leads, calls, conversations, messages/media references, appointments, holds, alerts, automation and webhook jobs, notification destinations/receipts, policies, settings, trial identity redemptions, integration credentials and tenant reporting projections. OAuth revocation and Twilio release do not delete provider-held history. Stripe retains its own legally required billing records; provider retention/export rights must be covered in the privacy notice and vendor agreements.

The application retains only minimal financial references (Stripe customer/subscription identifiers, plan, price and dates) for 2,557 days, and anonymous completion counts for 90 days. Completed billable-number release receipts are unlinkably anonymized: business ID replaced, provider account/resource identifiers hashed, authorization references and claim secrets removed; these financial release proofs expire after 2,557 days. No caller content, phone, address, email or owner mapping is retained in these records. The financial retention period is a conservative operational policy, not a determination of applicable tax law; the operator must confirm the required period with counsel/accounting before launch.

Erasure intentionally removes trial identity keys. One-trial uniqueness remains enforced for retained accounts; the service cannot recognize an erased identity. The supervised paid pilot must not recycle deleted identities through new free trials. A permanent public self-service anti-abuse policy requires a separate approved retention/legal basis.

Voice transcripts/summaries on terminal sessions are automatically redacted after 30 days by the leased privacy worker. Stale nonterminal voice sessions are reaped after 15 minutes. This is content redaction, not whole-record erasure. Other customer records remain until account erasure; there is no implied short TTL for leads or appointment history.

Backups: an erasure must be recorded in the operator's deletion register and reapplied after a restore before traffic is reopened. This repository does not prove deployed backup retention or a completed restore rehearsal. Restrict backup access, set an explicit expiry, and verify restoration with synthetic tenants before the pilot. No legal hold is silently inferred by this code.
